The best task manager apps for iPhone

There isn't one best to-do app. There's the one that fits how your brain works, and most people go through three or four before they find it. Here are seven worth trying.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Short answer

If you'd like an app that simply tells you what to do next, with no setup, account or subscription, try Taven. If you love beautiful design and would rather pay once than subscribe, get Things 3. If you're on Android or Windows too, or you share projects with people, go with Todoist. If you want a timer, calendar and habit tracker all in one place, look at TickTick. And if you just need a list, Apple Reminders is already on your phone.

Quick disclosure: we make Taven, so take our enthusiasm with a grain of salt. That said, every app on this page is good at something, and we've tried to say plainly where Taven isn't the right pick. Prices and features change a lot, so double check the App Store before you commit to anything.

How we picked these

We tried to judge these the way you'd actually use them. How quickly can you get a task in? Does the app help you decide what to work on, or does it just hold your list? Does it work on all your devices? What will it cost you over a year? And what happens to your data?

Every app here is actively maintained and good at something. The trick is matching the app to the way you work, rather than the other way round.

The apps

Taven Best for knowing what's next

Best for
People who don't want to run a productivity system. You open it, it tells you what to do next, you do it.
Platforms
iPhone and iPad
Price
Free. No subscription and no account.
What's good
The AI picks your top task and tells you why (something like “Due today and your top priority”), and it does this on your phone rather than on a server. It also has a clean, minimal design, a calendar, recurring tasks that double as habits with streaks, and some genuinely nice completion stats. Sync between devices goes through your own iCloud if you want it.
The catch
It's Apple only. There's no team collaboration or shared projects, and you won't find the deep integrations or power user views that older apps have built up over the years.

Things 3 Best design

Best for
Individuals who are all in on Apple and want something calm and gorgeous.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch
Price
You pay once, but separately for iPhone, iPad and Mac.
What's good
It's probably the best looking task app ever made. The Today, Upcoming, Anytime and Someday structure is clever, quick entry is fast, and syncing through Things Cloud is free.
The catch
No collaboration, nothing for Android or Windows, and while it organizes your tasks beautifully, deciding what to actually do is still your job.

Todoist Best cross-platform

Best for
People who bounce between iPhone, Android, Windows and a browser, or who share projects with others.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, web, browser extensions
Price
There's a free plan with limits, plus paid Pro and Business plans.
What's good
Its natural language entry is the best around. Type “pay rent every 1st” and it just works. Add labels, filters, shared projects, comments, a huge pile of integrations and some AI features, and you can build almost anything.
The catch
You need an account, and your tasks live on Todoist's servers. The free plan is fairly limited, and the really powerful filters take a while to set up.

TickTick Best all-in-one

Best for
People who'd like their tasks, calendar, habit tracker and focus timer all in the same app.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, web
Price
Free plan, with a paid Premium subscription.
What's good
There's a built-in Pomodoro timer, a habit tracker, calendar views, an Eisenhower matrix view and natural language entry. It's a lot of app for the money.
The catch
You need an account, and plenty of the best bits (like the full calendar views) are Premium only. All those features also make for a busier screen.

Apple Reminders Best free built-in

Best for
Simple personal and household lists, especially ones you share with family.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, iCloud.com
Price
Free.
What's good
It's already installed, Siri handles it well, and shared lists, smart lists, tags and the grocery list sorting are all genuinely useful.
The catch
It holds your list but won't help you prioritize it, and the stats and habit tracking are pretty basic.

Microsoft To Do Best for Microsoft people

Best for
Anyone whose working life runs through Outlook and Microsoft 365.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, web
Price
Free.
What's good
The “My Day” list is a nice daily planning habit, and it syncs with your Outlook tasks. Shared lists work well too.
The catch
You'll need a Microsoft account, and it has fewer advanced features than Todoist or TickTick.

OmniFocus Best for GTD power users

Best for
People who follow Getting Things Done and want total control over how they see their work.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, web
Price
Subscription or license.
What's good
Custom perspectives, defer dates, a proper weekly review mode and deep automation.
The catch
The learning curve is steep and it's the priciest app here. For most people, it's a lot more than they need.

So which one should you get?

If you've got too many tasks and you never know where to start, that's exactly the problem Taven was built for. It's organized around one question: what should I do next? (We wrote a whole guide on how to decide what task to do next if you'd like to do it by hand.)

If you also use Android or Windows, you'll want Todoist or TickTick. If you share lists with a team or a partner, Todoist is the strongest choice, though Apple Reminders is fine for simple shared lists. If you hate subscriptions and care a lot about design, Things 3 is lovely.

If a focus timer matters to you, TickTick has one built in. Taven has a calendar and habit streaks, but no Pomodoro timer. And if privacy is your top priority, Taven (no account, AI on your device) and Things 3 are the two to look at.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best free task manager app for iPhone?

If you want help prioritizing plus habit tracking, Taven is free with no subscription or account. If you just need simple lists, Apple Reminders is free and already on your phone. Todoist and TickTick both have free plans too, with more behind a subscription.

What's the best to-do app for both iPhone and iPad?

Taven, Things 3, Todoist, TickTick and OmniFocus all work on both. Taven syncs between them through your own private iCloud account.

Which iPhone task manager doesn't need an account?

Taven doesn't need one at all, and it does all its processing on your device. Things 3 also works without an account unless you turn on Things Cloud sync.

Which to-do app tells you what to do next?

That's Taven's whole thing. It looks at importance, due dates and how often a task repeats, then highlights the one task you should do next and tells you why.
