The best task manager apps for iPhone

There's no single best to-do app — there's a best app for how you work. Here are seven strong iPhone options, who each is for, and where each one falls short.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Short answer

If you want an app that tells you what to do next with no setup, account or subscription, try Taven. If you want a polished, Apple-only planner you buy once, choose Things 3. If you need cross-platform sync and shared projects, choose Todoist. If you want a calendar, Pomodoro timer and habits in one app, choose TickTick. If you just need simple free lists, Apple Reminders is already on your phone.

Disclosure: this page is published by the makers of Taven. We've tried to be fair — every app here is genuinely good at something, and we say plainly where Taven isn't the best fit. App features and pricing change often; check each app's App Store page for current details.

How we chose

We looked at what actually matters day to day on an iPhone: how fast you can capture a task, how well the app helps you decide what to work on, whether it works across your devices, what it costs over a year, and what it does with your data. Every app below is actively maintained and genuinely good — the right pick depends on what you need.

The apps

Taven Best for deciding what to do next

Best for
People who don't want to manage a system — they want to open the app and be told what to do next.
Platforms
iPhone and iPad
Price
Free. No subscription, no in-app account.
Strengths
On-device AI picks your top task and explains why (“Due today and your top priority”). Smart recurrence for daily, weekly, monthly and yearly tasks, habit streaks, completion analytics, optional iCloud sync. No account, no tracking, no third-party SDKs.
Trade-offs
Apple devices only. No team collaboration or shared projects. Fewer integrations and power-user views than long-established apps.

Things 3 Best design

Best for
Individuals who want a beautiful, calm planner and are fully in the Apple ecosystem.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch
Price
One-time purchase, sold separately for iPhone, iPad and Mac.
Strengths
Exceptional design, Today/Upcoming/Anytime/Someday structure, projects with headings, fast quick-entry, free Things Cloud sync.
Trade-offs
No collaboration, no Android or Windows, and it organizes tasks beautifully but leaves deciding what to do next to you.

Todoist Best cross-platform

Best for
People who work across iPhone, Android, Windows and the web, or share projects with others.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, web, browser extensions
Price
Free plan with limits; paid Pro and Business plans.
Strengths
Best-in-class natural-language quick add (“pay rent every 1st”), labels and filters, shared projects, comments, hundreds of integrations, AI features.
Trade-offs
Requires an account and stores your tasks on its servers. The free plan is limited. Powerful filters take time to set up.

TickTick Best all-in-one

Best for
People who want tasks, a calendar, a habit tracker and a focus timer in one app.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, web
Price
Free plan; paid Premium subscription.
Strengths
Built-in Pomodoro focus timer, habit tracker, calendar views, an Eisenhower matrix view, natural-language entry.
Trade-offs
Requires an account. Many of its best features (like full calendar views) are Premium. Lots of features means a busier interface.

Apple Reminders Best free built-in

Best for
Simple personal and household lists, especially shared with family.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, iCloud.com
Price
Free.
Strengths
Already installed, great Siri integration, shared lists, grocery list sorting, smart lists, tags.
Trade-offs
Doesn't help you prioritize, and analytics and habit tracking are basic.

Microsoft To Do Best for Microsoft users

Best for
People who live in Outlook and Microsoft 365.
Platforms
iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, web
Price
Free.
Strengths
“My Day” daily planning list, syncs with Outlook tasks, shared lists.
Trade-offs
Requires a Microsoft account. Fewer advanced features than Todoist or TickTick.

OmniFocus Best for GTD power users

Best for
People who follow Getting Things Done and want maximum control.
Platforms
iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, web
Price
Subscription or license.
Strengths
Perspectives (custom views), defer dates, review mode, deep automation.
Trade-offs
Steep learning curve and the highest price on this list. Overkill for most people.

Which one should you pick?

  • You have too many tasks and don't know where to start: Taven. It's built around one question — what should I do next? See also our guide on how to decide what task to do next.
  • You use Android or Windows too: Todoist or TickTick.
  • You share task lists with a team or partner: Todoist, or Apple Reminders for simple shared lists.
  • You want to buy once, not subscribe, and love design: Things 3.
  • You want a focus timer and habit tracker built in: TickTick (Taven also tracks habits and streaks, but has no Pomodoro timer).
  • Privacy matters most: Taven (no account, on-device AI) or Things 3.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free task manager app for iPhone?

If you want prioritization and habit tracking, Taven is free with no subscription or account. If you only need simple lists, Apple Reminders is free and built in. Todoist and TickTick both have free plans, with more features behind a subscription.

What is the best to-do app for iPhone and iPad?

Taven, Things 3, Todoist, TickTick and OmniFocus all work on both iPhone and iPad. Taven syncs between them through your private iCloud account.

Which iPhone task manager doesn't require an account?

Taven doesn't require any account and processes everything on your device. Things 3 also works without an account unless you turn on Things Cloud sync.

Which to-do app tells you what to do next?

Taven is built specifically for this: its on-device AI weighs importance, due dates and recurrence, then highlights your top task with a short reason.
