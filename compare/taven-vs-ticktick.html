Taven vs. TickTick

TickTick gives you lots of ways to look at your tasks. Taven gives you one answer: here's what to do next. Here's how the two compare, and why people who feel a bit buried by features end up switching.

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Short answer

If you're on iPhone or iPad and want a calm app that tells you what to do next rather than handing you more views to manage, Taven is the better pick. It's free with no Premium tier, has no account, includes a calendar, gives every recurring task its own habit streak, and runs its AI privately on your device. TickTick makes more sense if you want a built-in Pomodoro timer, or you need Android, Windows or the web.

Quick disclosure: Taven is our app, so yes, we're biased. We've still kept every fact on this page accurate, and we've pointed out where TickTick is the better choice. Features and prices change often, so check the App Store for the latest.

Why you might choose Taven over TickTick

1. One answer instead of more views

TickTick gives you lists, a calendar, an Eisenhower matrix, a habit tracker and a focus timer. That's powerful, but every one of those views is another place to check and another decision to make. Taven's on-device AI looks at importance, due dates and repeating tasks, then puts one AI Suggested task at the top with the reason why. Less looking around, more getting on with it.

2. Free, with no Premium paywall

Taven is free and there's no subscription. Quite a few of TickTick's best features, including its full calendar views, need a Premium subscription. Taven's calendar comes included.

3. Private by design

Taven has no accounts, no servers holding your tasks and no third-party SDKs, and it doesn't collect any data. The AI runs on your device. TickTick needs an account and syncs your tasks through its cloud.

4. Habits that live inside your tasks

In TickTick, habits sit in a separate tracker that you maintain alongside your to-do list. In Taven, a recurring task is the habit. It gets its own streak and history automatically, and you can skip a day without losing track.

5. A calmer app

Having fewer features is kind of the point. There's less to set up, fewer corners for tasks to hide in, and nothing pulling you away from the one thing you're meant to be doing.

6. Stats that keep you honest

Completion rings, weekly charts, monthly heatmaps and your best day, plus a “Today's Intensity” gauge that lets you know when you've planned more than you can finish.

A typical morning in each app

TickTick

  1. Open the app
  2. Choose a view: list, calendar or matrix
  3. Check your habits over in the habit tracker
  4. Work out what's urgent and what's important
  5. Pick a task and start a focus timer

Taven

  1. Open the app
  2. See your AI Suggested task and why it's first
  3. Start it. Your habits are already in your task list

When TickTick might suit you better

TickTick is a really capable all-in-one app, and it's the better choice in a few situations. If you want a Pomodoro focus timer inside your task app, TickTick has one and Taven doesn't. If you need Android, Windows or the web, TickTick works there and Taven is iPhone and iPad only. And if you share lists with other people, TickTick supports that.

But if what you really want is to stop deliberating and start working, Taven's focus on one clear next step is the whole advantage. You can also see what Taven users love most.

Moving from TickTick to Taven

  1. Download Taven. It's free and there's no account to set up.
  2. Add whatever's due in the next week first. There's no need to move your whole backlog on day one.
  3. Turn each of your TickTick habits into a recurring task in Taven. It'll pick up a streak automatically.
  4. Switch on iCloud sync if you use both an iPhone and an iPad.
  5. Tomorrow morning, open Taven and do whatever it suggests.

The verdict

Go with Taven if…

  • You're on iPhone or iPad
  • Too many views and features make you procrastinate
  • You'd like to be told what to do next
  • You want privacy and no subscription
  • You'd like your habits tracked inside your tasks

Stick with TickTick if…

  • You rely on a built-in Pomodoro timer
  • You need Android, Windows or web access
  • You share lists with other people

Frequently asked questions

Is Taven a good alternative to TickTick?

Yes, especially if you're on iPhone or iPad and feel like TickTick has more features than you actually use. Taven is free with no Premium tier, doesn't need an account, keeps your data on your device, includes a calendar, builds habit streaks into recurring tasks, and tells you what to do next.

Why do people switch from TickTick to Taven?

Usually because they want something simpler that makes the prioritizing decision for them. Privacy is another big one (no account, on-device AI, no data collected), and so is not having to pay for Premium.

Does Taven have a habit tracker?

It does. Every recurring task in Taven, whether daily, weekly, monthly or yearly, becomes a habit with its own streak and history, and you can skip a day without losing track.

Does Taven have a Pomodoro timer?

No. Taven focuses on helping you pick the right task to work on. If a built-in focus timer is a must for you, TickTick has one.
